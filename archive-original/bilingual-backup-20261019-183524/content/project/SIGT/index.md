## The question

How do people balance a trustworthy-looking face against evidence from actual outcomes?

## My contribution

I contributed to the research program on trust-related decision-making and learning across adulthood. My portfolio includes work on the Social Iowa Gambling Task, and I coauthored the public explanation of the age-group study.

## The approach

The age-group study compared the standard Iowa Gambling Task with a social version pairing card decks with faces. It included 143 younger and 129 older adults, allowing the team to examine choices when facial impressions and payouts agreed or conflicted.

## What came out of it

Both groups initially favored trustworthy-looking faces. Older adults had more difficulty adapting when those impressions conflicted with the outcomes. The findings highlight the importance of considering both social cues and feedback when studying trust.
